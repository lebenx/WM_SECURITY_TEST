## Abstract

Instruction-conditioned robot world models often place task text, observations, actions, and imagined futures in one spatiotemporal Transformer. We study a legal instruction-switch intervention: the current image, proprioception, executed action chunk, reward, labels, weights, and gradients remain fixed, while the attacker selects another supported task instruction. Our claim is that instruction-conditioned world models can write task text into a shared action-token causal bus, allowing the goal to change a physical future forecast even when direct goal-to-world edges are masked. A matched split of this bus restores goal-invariant world prediction while retaining goal-conditioned policy outputs.

The evidence spans two released carriers. On WorldAgen, 30 real LIBERO-10 windows from 10 episodes show a native goal-swap world relative L2 of 0.2999; cutting the nine native goal-to-input-action edges gives exact world invariance in 30/30 windows while the policy relative L2 remains 0.1760. A same-episode alternate-action control across 30 windows changes the repaired world output in every window (mean relative L2 0.1655, cluster-bootstrap CI [0.1469, 0.1855]). On AlphaBrain CosmosPolicy, an expanded held-out matrix covers 21 fixed image/state/recorded-action windows from seven real episodes. Native goal swaps are nonzero in all 21 windows (mean 0.05317, episode-cluster bootstrap 95% CI [0.04512, 0.06180]); the corrected fixed-action world cut is exactly invariant in all 21. The same cut also preserves action-to-world sensitivity in a three-window alternate-action control (mean 0.08715). A seed-1 held-out repair MSE increase of 0.04899 is conditional: independent noise seeds 2 and 3 preserve exact invariance but produce mean repair deltas of −0.00079 and −0.03820. Thus invariance and action sensitivity are the stable mechanism evidence; forecast-fidelity direction is sampler-dependent.
An additional WorldAgen activation mediation patch injects recorded-goal action-token states into swapped-goal forwards across 21 windows (seven episodes at frames 30, 60, and 90): layer 4/8 world residuals fall to 0.2062/0.1998 (cluster-bootstrap CIs [0.1678, 0.2482] and [0.1796, 0.2202]), with movement toward the recorded goal in all 21 windows, while policy residuals remain 0.9555/0.9643.

The released WorldAgen and AlphaBrain checkpoints do not pass the LIBERO clean-task gate, and their native policies do not consume the World Forecast for action selection. We therefore make no native task-harm or closed-loop attack claim. In a separate, explicitly labeled forecast-driven planner probe, the same WorldAgen forecast changes candidate ranking and, under a Clean-calibrated 30-step replay, can increase candidate regret and reduce EEF progress; the direction still reverses under a second fixed scorer and across held-out task splits. The current result is therefore a cross-carrier forecast mechanism plus planner-dependent decision perturbation, with task-capable clean baselines and a trained goal-free dynamics head still required for a task-level conclusion.

As a cross-carrier feasibility gate, we also ran one AlphaBrain forecast-driven planner pilot with task 0/seed 17, eight frozen action candidates, a fixed initial state, and 30-step LIBERO replays. A CLIP score on the future-primary forecast selected candidate 4 under Clean (regret 0.0621 m) and candidate 6 under Goal-Swap (regret 0.4228 m), where utility is read only from the real replay. The fixed-action Bus-Cut made Clean and Swap scores exactly identical but selected candidate 6 in both cases, so it did not recover the Clean decision. This single-task pilot is recorded as a negative repair result and a feasibility gate only; it does not establish cross-carrier task harm or a universal decision repair, and the scorer is not calibrated enough to replace the WorldAgen main result ([artifact](../runs/alphabrain_forecast_planner_v0/summary.json), [script](../scripts/pilot_alphabrain_forecast_planner.py)).

We then extended the same protocol to tasks 0/2/4 with the same seed 17, eight frozen candidates per task, a 16-step forecast, and 30-step replay. Goal-Swap changed the selected action in 2/3 tasks. Mean regret was 0.1836 m for Clean native, 0.2251 m for Goal-Swap, and 0.2796 m for fixed-action Bus-Cut; Clean and Swap cut scores were exactly identical task by task. The direction was task-dependent: on task 4, Goal-Swap reduced regret from 0.3765 m to 0.1402 m. Bus-Cut recovered the Clean action in 1/3 tasks. This supports cross-carrier planner reachability, not universal utility harm, native-policy damage, or task-success loss ([artifact](../runs/alphabrain_forecast_planner_multitask_v1/summary.json)).

To avoid treating the three-task pilot as the main result, we extended the same protocol to the six tasks used by the WorldAgen sweep (0/2/4/6/7/9), still with seed 17 and the fixed candidate, scorer, and replay protocol. Goal-Swap changed the selected action in 5/6 tasks. Mean regret was 0.1646 m for Clean, 0.1911 m for Goal-Swap, and 0.2433 m for Bus-Cut; tasks 4 and 6 improved under the swap while tasks 0, 7, and 9 worsened, giving a net difference of only +0.0265 m. This strengthens cross-carrier planner reachability while directly ruling out universal utility harm and complete decision repair ([artifact](../runs/alphabrain_forecast_planner_six_task_v1/summary.json)).

Goal-Swap changed 15/18 selected actions. Mean Clean, Swap, and Bus-Cut regret was 0.1511/0.1885/0.2607 m, with Swap minus Clean +0.0374 m. The initial-state-cluster bootstrap interval was [+0.0098,+0.0758] m, whereas the task-cluster interval was [-0.1086,+0.1931] m. The effect is descriptive across states, not a universal cross-task harm result. ## 1. Threat model and invariant

The experimenter changes only the preference text supplied to a forecast query, denoted $G_{query}$. The original task $G_{task}$, policy and value objectives, success criterion, all non-language model inputs and history, committed low-level actions, weights, inference configuration, and randomness remain fixed. A branch-specific experimental intervention is not evidence that a deployed attacker can write that field. Text drawn from the task corpus is also not automatically compatible with the fixed physical scene.

The physical premise concerns an intervention. Let $s$ include the simulator, controller, scene, and relevant sensor state; let $a$ specify every executed low-level control and its duration over the complete forecast horizon; and let $u$ be the paired exogenous noise. If query Goal is preference-only metadata and does not enter the physical transition, then $Y^{phys}(s,do(A=a),do(G_{query}=g_1),u)=Y^{phys}(s,do(A=a),do(G_{query}=g_2),u)$. Changing subsequent controls, scene reset, dynamics, another agent's behavior, or execution horizon invalidates this premise. Without paired noise, the claim concerns equal outcome distributions, not identical samples. Observing a feedback-selected action sequence does not implement $do(A=a)$.

The model may observe only a history $H$ of images, proprioception, previous actions, time indices, and cache state. Goal can convey hidden-state information through either explicit statements or implicit training correlations. Consequently, physical invariance does not imply that the observational predictor $P_{train}(Y\mid H,A,G_{query})$ must ignore Goal. The complete observational identity retains all conditions: $P_{train}(Y\mid H,A,G_{query})=\int P_{train}(Y\mid S,H,A,G_{query})P_{train}(S\mid H,A,G_{query})dS$. Feedback-selected actions can additionally select hidden states or future disturbances. Identical scenes and exact environment replays do not establish observation sufficiency or training support for crossed Goal queries.

WorldAgen provides a narrower, model-specific basis: its [official method](https://worldagen.github.io/#method) describes a task-agnostic world head and instruction isolation. The [paper](https://worldagen.github.io/worldagen_paper.pdf), Sections 2.2 and 2.4, supports that interpretation, although Section 2.6 also mentions instruction conditioning. We therefore audit the explicit architecture contract, not a universal physical obligation of language-conditioned predictors. The input action values can remain fixed while their hidden representations acquire Goal information through shared Transformer layers; this permits a Goal-to-action-hidden-to-world path despite blocked direct text-to-world edges. AlphaBrain and Cosmos Policy do not inherit this isolation promise.

The current research questions are separate: does an interface satisfy its stated instruction-isolation contract; does query dependence increase error against independently executed physical outcomes; and does it harm real decisions with a beneficial, controlled repair? Randomizing or fully crossing the same preregistered compatible Goal pool independently of hidden state defines a useful intervention distribution, but does not prove training-distribution support or impose a new contract on an observational model. The corrected WorldAgen comparison covers only one independent episode: same-scene Goal-Swap increases mean native RGB-patch MSE from 0.055437 to 0.067874 over six correlated transitions. This supports a bounded isolation/error result, not general decision harm. Forecast divergence, exact invariance after a cut, or action sensitivity alone is insufficient to answer the latter questions. The experimental criteria retained below are historical mechanism diagnostics, not current attack-success criteria.

### 1.1 Estimands and falsification criteria

Let the frozen model's world forecast under image, state, executed action, instruction `g`, and path intervention `m` be `F_θ(x,s,u,g;m)`, and its policy output be `P_θ(x,s,u,g;m)`. For two legal instructions `g,g'`, the fixed-physical-input goal effect is `Δ_g^W(m) = d(F_θ(x,s,u,g;m), F_θ(x,s,u,g';m))`. For two same-episode executed action chunks `u,u'` under one instruction, action sensitivity is `Δ_u^W(m) = d(F_θ(x,s,u,g;m), F_θ(x,s,u',g;m))`. The distance `d` is the relative L2 or latent distance recorded by each carrier; values across carriers are not treated as one common scale.

For a WorldAgen layer-`l` activation patch, `B←A;l` denotes a swapped-goal `B` forward in which the recorded-goal `A` action-token hidden state is injected. We report `ρ_l^W = d(F_{B←A;l},F_A)/d(F_B,F_A)` and the analogous policy residual `ρ_l^P`. A localized pattern with `ρ_l^W<1` and `ρ_l^P≈1` supports mediation through the intermediate action state; it is an internal localization diagnostic, not an attacker capability.

The primary criteria are native `Δ_g^W>0`, recovery to `Δ_g^W≈0` under the matched fixed-action cut, and retained policy goal sensitivity or alternate-action `Δ_u^W`. Held-out forecast MSE repair is a secondary fidelity estimand interpreted only at fixed sampler/noise settings. The claim is falsified by native goal invariance, failure of the cut to restore invariance, loss of real action sensitivity after the cut, or absence of localized recovery around the intermediate action state.

## 2. Related work and positioning

[WorldVLA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.21539) unifies action and image generation and uses an attention mask to reduce prior-action error propagation. [Causal World Modeling for Robot Control](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.21998) uses a shared latent space for vision and action tokens with closed-loop execution. [AffordanceWAM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22332) separates World and Action Experts and couples them through masked joint self-attention, while [When to Trust Imagination](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06222) verifies imagined futures against real observations to adapt action chunks. These works motivate structured world-action pathways, but they do not formulate legal task-text changes under fixed physical state and executed action as a security invariant or test a frozen-weight edge split that restores it.

Our claim is broader than a single prompt-leak example but narrower than a universal statement about all world-action models: existing architectures can expose a security-relevant intermediate action-token bus through which legal task text changes a fixed-state/fixed-action world forecast. The direct-world cut, carrier cut, and alternate-action control distinguish this mechanism from direct language conditioning, ordinary action-mask design, and parameter-update interference. A focused [related-work memo](related_work_goal_bus.md) records the boundary.

## 3. Mechanism hypothesis

In WorldAgen's native attention mask, the goal has no direct edge to the world-output token. It can nevertheless write a contextualized input-action token, which can then write the future-world token. We call this two-hop route a shared action-token causal bus. Cutting the nine goal-to-input-action edges removes the route to the world branch while leaving the policy branch goal-sensitive.

AlphaBrain CosmosPolicy provides an independent carrier. A T5-conditioned DiT jointly produces action, future proprioception, future wrist, future primary-image, and value latent slots. We apply a matched row-wise cut: condition text is removed from the action/value carriers and the world rows, while world rows retain self-attention to the recorded action carrier. Images, state, recorded actions, condition masks, time steps, and sampler noise are held fixed.

A causal ladder localizes the mediator. Removing only the world rows' direct text cross-attention leaves a nonzero future-world difference (mean 0.04235, 3/3 windows). Removing text from the action/value carriers as well restores exact fixed-action invariance. The result is consistent with text first entering an action carrier and then being read by the world branch, rather than with a direct world-row text leak.

### Activation mediation by layer

To move beyond edge necessity, we run an activation patch across WorldAgen's 24-layer Transformer. On 21 fixed real windows (seven episodes at frames 30, 60, and 90), we first record the action-token hidden states for recorded goal A. We then rerun the identical image, state, executed-action, and swapped-goal B input, replacing only the two chunks' input-action hidden states at one selected layer with A's states. The patch is an internal diagnostic rather than an attacker capability. We report a residual ratio: the patched-B-to-A output distance divided by the native B-to-A distance, separately for world and policy outputs.

At layers 4 and 8, the mean world residual falls to 0.2062 (cluster-bootstrap CI [0.1678, 0.2482]) and 0.1998 ([0.1796, 0.2202]), with movement toward A in all 21 windows, while the policy residual remains 0.9555 and 0.9643. An initial seven-window layer sweep gives residuals 0.8898, 0.4851, 0.6916, and 0.8723 at layers 0, 12, 16, and 20; patching the final layer has no downstream effect. The layer-localized pattern—strong world restoration from a mid-network action-carrier patch with the policy branch remaining B-sensitive—is consistent with mediation through the shared action-token bus and complements the direct edge-cut result. See the [21-window aggregate](../runs/worldagen_goal_bus_activation_patch_v1/summary.json) and [layer plot](figures/worldagen_activation_mediation.svg).

### 4.1 WorldAgen comparator

On 30 public LIBERO-10 windows, the native goal swap changes the world output by relative L2 0.2999. The explicit empty-goal comparator is 0.2704. The edge-cut world output is exactly invariant in every window, while the policy remains goal-sensitive. Recorded-goal, swapped-goal, and edge-cut primary forecast MSEs are 1.4228, 1.4960, and 1.4841; the swapped-minus-recorded MSE bootstrap CI is [0.0465, 0.1030].

As a matched specificity control, we delete nine finite goal edges of the same cardinality but target the future-action policy rows rather than the input-action carrier. The policy-only cut leaves the world goal-swap relative L2 at 0.2999 in all 30 windows, whereas the proposed carrier cut has exact-zero world difference; the control therefore rules out edge-count deletion as the explanation. See the [matched edge-cut aggregate](../runs/worldagen_goal_bus_edge_cut_controls_v0/summary.json).

Crossing the same 30 physical windows with all nine legal task offsets gives 270 paired rows. Native world relative L2 averages 0.3247 with a physical-window cluster-bootstrap CI [0.2860, 0.3613]; all 270 corrected world outputs are exactly invariant, while policy relative L2 averages 0.2000. The swapped-minus-recorded primary MSE delta averages 0.0779 with CI [0.0626, 0.0940]. Repeated offsets expand instruction coverage but are not counted as independent physical worlds.

As a matched physical-action control, we keep the current image, state, and recorded instruction fixed and replace only the six-step action chunk with another real chunk from the same episode. Across 30 windows (10 episodes, frames 30/60/90), native world sensitivity is 0.1623 (episode-cluster CI [0.1426, 0.1839]) and the nine-edge cut remains action-sensitive at 0.1655 (CI [0.1469, 0.1855]); all 30 repaired differences are nonzero. The cut therefore removes instruction dependence without freezing the WorldAgen dynamics response to executed actions. See the [action-control aggregate](../runs/worldagen_goal_bus_action_control_v1/summary.json).

The paired action-control and six-task-group results are plotted in [this figure](figures/worldagen_action_control.svg).

Leave-one-offset-out stability gives native means between 0.31685 and 0.33044 after omitting any single offset. Across offsets, the smallest native mean is 0.27854 and the largest is 0.38725, while corrected world outputs remain exactly invariant at every offset. The effect is therefore not driven by one semantic distance.

Grouping the same 30 windows by recorded legal task text yields six task groups (3--9 windows each). Every group has nonzero native world sensitivity and exact-zero cut output; group native means range from 0.0972 to 0.5792. This is descriptive task coverage, with repeated time points interpreted within their physical episodes. See the [task-group aggregate](../runs/worldagen_goal_bus_task_groups_v0/summary.json).

### Same-scene Goal crossing

To rule out a simulator or scene-definition confound, we crossed all ten legal LIBERO-Goal texts with the same physical BDDL scene, three complete initial simulator states, and four fixed action sequences. After removing language, goal predicates, and object-of-interest fields, the ten BDDL definitions have the same physical scene. Across 120 fresh replays, initial simulator state, qpos/qvel/contact, and the three-step future observation were exact in 120/120 cases. This is an environment-side conditional-invariance control: the Goal label did not change the true physical future.

On those same fixed inputs, native WorldAgen forecast Goal effects averaged 0.4913 MSE for non-Clean goals and were nonzero in every case. The matched Bus-Cut had maximum cross-goal variance 6.2e-15 and retained nonzero action sensitivity. Cell-level target-MSE changes remained mixed-sign (mean +0.0869, range -0.1610 to +0.2006). Averaging uniformly sampled non-Clean legal Goals within each shared initial-state cluster gives positive deltas for all three states (+0.0615/+0.1260/+0.1021), overall +0.0965 with a state-cluster bootstrap interval [+0.0615,+0.1260]. This is descriptive evidence that the Clean Goal is not an unbiased physical-target comparator, not a claim that every Goal worsens every forecast. Although Bus-Cut restores cross-Goal invariance, its baseline target MSE is +0.0884 above native Clean on average (+0.0530/+0.1425/+0.0696 by state), showing that forecast repair can carry a fidelity cost ([summary](../runs/worldagen_same_scene_goal_v1/summary.json), [analysis](../runs/worldagen_same_scene_goal_v1/analysis.json), [script](../scripts/pilot_worldagen_same_scene_goal.py), [figure](figures/worldagen_same_scene_goal_target_delta.svg)).

We then extended the empty-instruction (`Goal=""`) prompt-independent diagnostic to all 50 legal initial simulator states and four fixed actions. All 200 physical target replays were exact. Relative to Clean, empty-Goal target MSE was higher in all 50 state clusters, with mean +0.0744 and a state-cluster bootstrap interval [+0.0665,+0.0824]; empty-versus-Clean forecast MSE was 0.2139. This shows that Clean text carries a stable task prior for this checkpoint, but the empty prompt is not a trained goal-free dynamics head and does not make Goal universally beneficial ([50-state summary](../runs/worldagen_same_scene_empty_goal_all50_v1/summary.json), [figure](figures/worldagen_empty_goal_comparator.svg), [three-state pilot](../runs/worldagen_same_scene_empty_goal_v1/summary.json), [script](../scripts/pilot_worldagen_same_scene_empty_goal.py)).

### 4.2 Fixed-action AlphaBrain replication

The public compatible Wan2.1 VAE encodes real LIBERO images into the Cosmos latent shape `[1,16,9,28,28]`. Every probe uses the recorded 16-step executed action chunk rather than a zero-action placeholder. Native future-world goal swaps are nonzero in all seven one-step windows and all nine legal-offset rows. The corrected action-bus cut is exactly invariant in every corresponding window, while action/value policy slots remain goal-sensitive.

The expanded seed-1 matrix uses three time points in each of seven real episodes. Native world differences are nonzero in all 21 windows and the corrected cut is exactly invariant in all 21. In the separate alternate-action control, replacing the recorded action chunk with another same-episode recorded chunk changes the repaired world output in all three windows (mean 0.08715). This rules out the interpretation that the cut simply erased physical action conditioning.

On the same three episodes at frame 30, the descriptive legal goal-swap world effect averages 0.07137 versus 0.08715 for the repaired alternate-action effect, a goal-to-action scale ratio of 0.819. This is an effect-size comparison rather than a three-episode significance test; it shows that the goal effect is on the scale of a real action intervention, not merely numerically nonzero. See the [effect-size aggregate](../runs/alphabrain_goal_vs_action_effect_v0/summary.json).

### 4.3 Forecast fidelity and sampler dependence

For each matrix window, the observed frame 16 steps later is encoded as a held-out target; the target is never supplied to the denoiser. Seed 1 gives a mean repair MSE delta of +0.04899 with an episode-cluster CI [0.03321, 0.06798], positive in all seven episode means. This is a secondary fidelity metric rather than the security invariant. Repeating fixed-action probes at noise seed 2 and seed 3 preserves exact repaired invariance, but gives negative repair deltas of −0.00079 and −0.03820. We therefore do not claim that repair universally increases forecast error.

AlphaBrain has no precomputed empty-instruction embedding. Zero T5 context is reported only as a prompt-independent diagnostic, not as a trained goal-free dynamics baseline. WorldAgen's empty-goal condition is the formal no-goal comparator.

Task-stratifying the matrix by task-A text gives five legal task groups. After adding the three seed-3 repeat windows, every group remains nonzero under the native goal swap and exactly invariant under the corrected cut; group native means are 0.04878, 0.04118, 0.04450, 0.12809, and 0.06945. This is descriptive core-task coverage, with repeated time points interpreted within physical episodes rather than as independent worlds.

### 4.4 A bounded learned goal-free-context comparator

To separate a zero-context diagnostic from a trainable comparator, we froze the released 2B DiT and VAE and optimized only one shared $512\times1024$ instruction-free context tensor. The initial short comparator used three frame-30 windows from episodes 3--5 for training and the disjoint episodes 6--9 for evaluation. Two independent seeds (17 and 23), each using eight steps at learning rate 0.001, reduced one-step future-world MSE from 0.58519 for zero context to 0.46215 and 0.46219 on the four held-out windows. As a short multi-timepoint check, we kept the same disjoint episode split and optimization budget but used frames 30, 60, and 90: nine training windows and twelve held-out windows. Seed 29 reduced held-out MSE from 0.60197 to 0.47852; the four episode-cluster delta means give a bootstrap 95\% CI of [-0.18414, -0.01299]. The same budget at learning rate 0.05 increased the held-out MSE to 1.47141, so this low-capacity comparator is optimization-sensitive.

This is evidence that a shared learned context can serve as a goal-free forecast comparator across multiple time points. It is not a retrained dynamics head, policy fine-tuning, environment rollout, or task-success result; the full matched goal-free dynamics baseline remains open.

### 4.6 Counterfactual action ranking and real consequences

The feasibility audit is negative for native decision use: WorldAgen emits its policy action before calling the World Forecast, and AlphaBrain decodes its action latent directly. We therefore evaluate decision consequences only with an explicitly separate independent planner. This planner freezes a set of eight recorded LIBERO action trajectories per task, scores each candidate using the WorldAgen primary-image forecast and the original clean goal text, and ignores the policy head output. The candidate set, score text, checkpoint, seed, initial state, and replay horizon are held fixed across Clean, Goal-Swap, Goal-Swap + Bus-Cut, and matched Control-Cut. Selected trajectories are executed in fresh LIBERO environments; utility is measured from simulator state, not the model score.

The reported Forecast MSE uses only the step-3 target for the first primary-view future chunk. Selection uses that forecast, while the selected trajectory is then replayed for 30 simulator steps for the real EEF consequence.

On six LIBERO-10 tasks (0, 2, 4, 6, 7, and 9), the global-minmax reconstruction scorer gives a Goal-Swap pairwise ranking flip rate of 0.4464 +/- 0.0774 and an EEF approach-progress difference of -0.0547 m relative to Clean. Bus-Cut gives +0.0154 m, while the matched Control-Cut is score-identical to Goal-Swap (maximum absolute score difference 0). Sparse reward and success are zero for all four conditions, so the utility result is an intermediate simulator consequence rather than task completion harm. A second LIBERO seed gives the same qualitative ranking perturbation; pooling 12 task-seed pairs gives flip 0.4435 +/- 0.1092 and EEF difference -0.0701 +/- 0.1493 m, with Bus-Cut +0.0216 +/- 0.1841 m. A physical-task cluster bootstrap gives EEF-difference CI [-0.1680, 0.0520] m, so we make no significance claim.
We then calibrated the planner with a strictly aligned protocol: only the first primary-view future chunk is scored, only the first three actions are replayed, and all eight candidates are replayed before utility is read. With a `reference_scalar` reconstruction using current-observation patch statistics, Clean regret is 0.0081 m versus 0.0131 m for a uniform random candidate; Goal-Swap offset 1 changes the selected action on 6/6 tasks with regret 0.0083 m, while offset 5 gives regret 0.0138 m and mean EEF progress -0.0005 m. The old global-minmax scorer fails this calibration gate (Clean regret 0.0200 m, above random), so its longer-horizon result is retained only as a scorer-specific ranking probe. See the [aligned planner summary](../runs/worldagen_aligned_planner_v1/summary.json), [statistics](../runs/worldagen_aligned_planner_v1/statistics.json), and [script](../scripts/pilot_worldagen_aligned_planner.py).

We repeated the aligned protocol with seeds 31 and 37, giving 18 task-seed pairs across three seeds. The `reference_scalar` Clean regret is 0.0087 +/- 0.0088 m versus 0.0131 +/- 0.0024 m for the random baseline; offset-1 regret is 0.0093 +/- 0.0080 m with EEF delta -0.0007 +/- 0.0066 m (task-cluster bootstrap CI [-0.0056, 0.0040]), and offset-5 regret is 0.0138 +/- 0.0091 m with EEF delta -0.0051 +/- 0.0105 m (CI [-0.0139, 0.0026]). Offset 1 and 5 change 17/18 and 13/18 selected actions, respectively. Bus-Cut has regret 0.0069 +/- 0.0042 m but changes the Clean action in 18/18 cases; matched Control-Cut score differences are exactly zero. These are descriptive task-clustered results, not significance claims. See the [three-seed aggregate](../runs/worldagen_aligned_planner_replicates_v1/statistics.json) and [aggregation script](../scripts/aggregate_worldagen_aligned_replicates.py).

The corresponding [replicate figure](figures/worldagen_aligned_planner_replicates.svg) reports regret, action changes, and task-clustered EEF intervals.

To measure longer real consequences without changing selection, we kept the step-3 forecast ranking but replayed the selected trajectory for 30 simulator steps across the same three seeds. With `reference_scalar`, Clean regret is 0.0758 +/- 0.1048 m versus 0.1345 +/- 0.0329 m for random; offset-1 regret is 0.1044 +/- 0.0412 m with EEF delta -0.0286 +/- 0.1302 m, while offset-5 regret is 0.1481 +/- 0.1102 m with EEF delta -0.0723 +/- 0.1570 m (task-cluster bootstrap CI [-0.1956, 0.0226]). Offset 5 therefore produces the larger real consequence under a Clean-calibrated long-horizon planner, although the interval still crosses zero. Bus-Cut reduces regret to 0.0373 +/- 0.0582 m and changes the Clean action in 18/18 cases, indicating a new planner baseline rather than clean-decision recovery. See the [30-step aggregate](../runs/worldagen_aligned_planner_h30_replicates_v1/statistics.json).

We expanded the legal Goal dose-response sweep to the six existing LIBERO tasks (0, 2, 4, 6, 7, and 9) and repeated it with seeds 17, 31, and 37, giving 18 task-seed pairs and 162 counterfactuals. In the three-seed aggregate, the `reference_scalar` EEF delta is negative for every offset, ranging from -0.0193 to -0.1330 m; offset 5 gives -0.0723 m EEF delta and 0.1481 m regret, exactly reproducing the earlier fixed-offset six-task long-replay result. Some individual tasks still have positive deltas, so the cross-task mean is not a universal single-task harm claim. Pairwise Action Ranking Flip Rate ranges from 0.448 to 0.556, and selected-action change from 0.722 to 1.000. This makes ranking perturbation and intermediate real consequences more stable than the two-task pilot, while still giving no task-success claim. Primary-view Forecast Error is not a monotonic dose measure: Clean is 1.9070 and offset 5 is 1.8621, so forecast error cannot substitute for decision consequences. A prespecified mediation diagnostic over the 162 cases finds pooled Spearman correlations of only 0.099 between Forecast Error change and ranking flip, -0.080 with EEF delta, and -0.080 with regret; the sign changes across offsets. Forecast Error therefore is not a sufficient proxy for decision harm. Bus-Cut selects the candidate with the best replayed EEF outcome in 12/18 task-seed pairs, with mean regret 0.0373 m, yet recovers the Clean selected action in 0/18 cases; it is therefore a partially effective planner-specific repair, not complete decision recovery. The earlier 54/54 two-task result is retained as a pilot and is not treated as the cross-task main result. See the [six-task three-seed legal-goal sweep](../runs/worldagen_aligned_goal_sweep_h30_all6_replicates_v1/statistics.json), [forecast-error mediation diagnostic](../runs/worldagen_aligned_goal_sweep_h30_all6_replicates_v1/mediation_statistics.json), [aggregation script](../scripts/aggregate_worldagen_aligned_goal_sweep.py), and [six-task dose-response figure](figures/worldagen_aligned_goal_sweep_all6_replicates.svg).

To directly test whether forecast repair and decision repair can be separated, we added a fixed repair path that reads no replay utility: candidate ranks from Clean and Bus-Cut are mixed with weights fixed in advance. Bus-Cut weight 1.0 gives regret 0.0373 m and zero Clean-action recovery; Clean weight 0.75 raises recovery to 0.778 with regret 0.0483 m; weight 0.25 lowers regret to 0.0291 m but recovers only 0.056 of Clean actions. This is an explicit utility/recovery Pareto rather than a single repair-success claim. It is a planner-level diagnostic requiring a Clean reference branch, not native-policy repair. See the [decision-repair path](../runs/worldagen_aligned_goal_sweep_h30_all6_replicates_v1/decision_repair_path.json), [repair-path script](../scripts/analyze_worldagen_decision_repair_path.py), and [repair-path figure](figures/worldagen_decision_repair_path.svg).
Candidate-cardinality sensitivity on the same replayed actions gives offset-1/5/8 regret of 0.1044/0.1481/0.2088 m for all eight candidates and 0.0915/0.1550/0.1940 m for a fixed four-candidate linspace subset. Pairwise ranking flip remains 0.546--0.583 in the four-candidate subset and matched Control-Cut remains score-identical. This is not a newly sampled four-candidate protocol, but it shows that the ranking perturbation is not an artifact of exactly eight candidates. See the [cardinality analysis](../runs/worldagen_aligned_goal_sweep_h30_all6_replicates_v1/candidate_cardinality.json) and [script](../scripts/analyze_worldagen_candidate_cardinality.py).
Before treating the divergence as a security event, we audited the conditional physical target. For every frozen candidate, the aligned sweep stores one real primary-view patch after the first three executed actions from the same initial simulator state; Clean, Goal-Swap, Bus-Cut, and Control-Cut only change the forecast branch. Across 18 task-seed pairs and 162 counterfactuals, recomputing target MSE reproduced the stored values with maximum absolute error 0. Goal-Swap latent divergence averaged 0.4603, while its physical-target MSE difference from Clean was mixed-sign: overall mean -0.0121 normalized MSE units with task-cluster bootstrap CI [-0.0434, 0.0181] normalized MSE units; offset 2 was -0.0581 and offset 3 was +0.0340. Spearman correlation between model-space divergence and physical-error change was only 0.117 (p=0.139). Control-Cut matched the corresponding Goal-Swap target MSE exactly, while Bus-Cut differed from Clean by -0.0144 normalized MSE units. This establishes the matched-target invariance audit, but not a universal physical-error increase: Goal changes the forecast under a fixed physical future, while the direction of target error depends on the offset. The target is a decoded primary-view patch after three actions, not a complete latent-state or task-success target. A post-hoc attempt to extract qpos/qvel/contact targets was invalidated: matching the reconstructed initial simulator hash did not reproduce stored EEF replays (maximum mismatch 0.3046 m) because controller/global RNG state was not persisted, so no reconstructed state target enters the analysis. Within the original ranking process, the repeat-3 fresh-environment audit has zero span for all recorded EEF/progress metrics; this establishes in-process determinism only. See the [physical-target audit](../runs/worldagen_aligned_goal_sweep_h30_all6_replicates_v1/goal_invariance_physical_audit.json) and [analysis script](../scripts/analyze_worldagen_goal_invariance_physical.py); the machine-readable [invariance specification](../configs/goal_bus_physical_invariance_spec.json) records allowed goal-sensitive channels and open partial-observability checks. The paired results are plotted in the [physical-target figure](figures/worldagen_goal_invariance_physical.svg).

To extend the physical target beyond pixels, we added an AlphaBrain CosmosPolicy future-output slot audit. We fixed 21 real image/state/recorded-action windows (seven episodes), the same observed +16-frame target, and the same sampler noise, then compared a legal Goal swap with the matched fixed-action world cut. Native Goal-Swap changed the future-proprio slot in 21/21 windows, with mean absolute latent delta 0.0607 and episode-cluster bootstrap CI [0.0376, 0.0970]; future-wrist and future-primary deltas were 0.0521 [0.0401, 0.0632] and 0.0462 [0.0303, 0.0687]. The fixed-action cut was exactly zero for all three slots in all 21 windows. Against the observed +16-frame target, however, the future-proprio MSE difference was +0.0084 with CI [-0.0139, 0.0325], future-wrist was +0.0248 with CI [0.0091, 0.0394], and the primary-image interval crossed zero. The fixed-action cut also costs target fidelity relative to native Clean: +0.0918 MSE on future-wrist (CI [0.0673, 0.1206]) and +0.0416 on future-primary (CI [0.0318, 0.0558]). This exposes the repair tradeoff directly: restoring invariance can reduce forecast fidelity. Extending the legal offset to 1, 5, and 9 gives 63 windows: the future-proprio absolute Goal effect is nonzero in 21/21 windows at each offset, with means 0.0512, 0.0607, and 0.0728; the fixed-action cut remains exactly zero in all three slots across all 63 windows. See the [AlphaBrain physical-slot audit](../runs/alphabrain_cosmos_policy_real_input_sampler_forecast_matrix_v1/physical_slot_audit.json), [offset extension](../runs/alphabrain_cosmos_policy_real_input_sampler_forecast_matrix_v1/physical_slot_offsets.json), [matrix](../runs/alphabrain_cosmos_policy_real_input_sampler_forecast_matrix_v1/summary.json), and [figure](figures/alphabrain_physical_slot_offsets.svg).

As a small semantic-surface control, we fixed image, state, recorded actions, masks, and RNG on three real windows while editing only the same task text's surface form. Double spaces and lowercasing were exactly invariant in all 3/3 native and Bus-Cut windows. Appending a period changed the native world output by relative L2 0.00259 on average (maximum 0.00279), while Bus-Cut remained exactly invariant in 3/3 windows. This is a text-surface fragility control, not physical error, attack success, or a paraphrase benchmark.

We then added a meaning-preserving paraphrase control. On the same three real windows, each task received two hand-written paraphrases while image, state, executed action, RNG, and attention mask were fixed. Native world relative L2 was nonzero for all six paraphrase pairs (mean 0.00844, maximum 0.01131); Bus-Cut was exactly invariant for all six, while policy outputs remained text-sensitive. The effect is much smaller than the legal Goal-Swap effect, so this control shows that the bus is not responding only to punctuation or whitespace, without turning paraphrase sensitivity into physical-harm evidence. To repair the state/contact-target reproducibility gap, we ran an isolated RNG-persisted audit without rerunning model inference. Across all six tasks (0, 2, 4, 6, 7, 9) and seeds 17, 31, and 37, each of 144 frozen candidates was replayed twice in a fresh environment after restoring Python/NumPy/Torch/CUDA RNG states. Initial simulator hashes, qpos, qvel, contact counts, three-step image hashes, EEF progress, and true progress matched exactly for 144/144 candidates (288 replay episodes). This validates the state-target persistence protocol across the full task-seed set, but adds no Goal effect or attack estimate. We then evaluated a forecast-only legal-Goal attacker: before reading any replay utility, it selects the offset with the largest pairwise ranking flip among offsets 1--9 using only model score vectors. The selected offset gives EEF delta -0.0995 m and regret 0.1753 m, compared with -0.0717 m and 0.1475 m for the paired uniform expectation; the task-cluster bootstrap CI for the regret increase is [-0.0230, 0.0786] m. Thus maximizing forecast/ranking divergence is not a reliable attack objective, which keeps Forecast Divergence separate from attack success. We also selected offsets directly from the saved WorldAgen \texttt{worlds_step3} tensors using swap-versus-Clean latent MSE, again before reading replay utility. Mean/max latent-MSE and raw-z-L1 selectors chose offset 9 in all three folds, giving EEF delta -0.0380 m and regret 0.1138 m versus a paired uniform-offset regret expectation of 0.1475 m (difference -0.0338 m, task-cluster CI [-0.1053, 0.0534] m). As a separate fixed-alpha cross-task ridge value probe, we fit only on Clean forecasts and real EEF outcomes from the training tasks, then selected actions on held-out tasks before reading their replay outcomes. Goal-Swap changed selections on 5/9 and 6/9 test task-seed cases in one split, and 3/9 and 4/9 in the complementary split for offsets 1 and 5. Clean calibration beat the random baseline in only one split, and the EEF direction reversed across splits. Reconstructing the forecast with the current observation patch's mean and variance, rather than a global min-max pseudo-image, keeps the candidate set, goal text, replay states, and utility definition unchanged but changes the six-task Goal-Swap EEF difference to +0.0149 m and Bus-Cut to +0.0381 m; ranking flip remains 0.4286. ## 5. Core-task boundary

We ran frozen control pilots on LIBERO spatial, object, and goal task families. WorldAgen native and edge-cut controls both obtain 0/3 successes at the official 600-step pilot; AlphaBrain native and corrected fixed-action controls both obtain 0/3 at the short 32-step pilot. A separate AlphaBrain spatial run to 608 steps is also 0/1 for both conditions. Action norms remain finite and comparable, but the released checkpoints do not pass the clean-task gate.

We then reran the same spatial initial state with the official CosmosPolicy JPEG/center-crop preprocessing, proprio min-max normalization, and five-step sampler. Native and fixed-action cut again obtain 0/1 at 608 steps, with mean action norms 0.25785 and 0.29310. Because the checkpoint configuration declares `dataset_mix: libero_goal`, we also evaluated matching Goal-suite tasks 0--2 for 304 steps each with the same official pipeline; native and fixed-action cut obtain 0/3 for both conditions, with mean action norms 0.36723 and 0.32465. The clean-task failure is therefore not explained by the earlier local preprocessing path or only by evaluating the Spatial suite; it remains a capability boundary rather than task-harm evidence.

These pilots are negative controls for immediate control collapse, not task success-rate or task-harm evidence. A task-level conclusion requires a clean task-capable checkpoint and a matched trained goal-free dynamics head. We do not infer closed-loop attack success from the forecast-level mechanism.

## 6. Limitations and conclusion

The evidence supports a cross-carrier prediction-level mechanism: legal task text can reach a physical future forecast through a shared action-token bus even when direct goal-to-world edges are masked; splitting the bus restores fixed-action world invariance while retaining policy and action sensitivity. When an independent forecast-driven planner consumes that forecast, the bus changes candidate ranking and, in a Clean-calibrated 30-step replay, can increase regret and reduce EEF progress. The direction of any forecast-fidelity or utility effect remains dependent on sampler, planner, and task split.

An additional sampled AlphaBrain activation patch on three windows did not reproduce WorldAgen's strong layer-localized return: action/value-carrier patches gave world residuals of roughly 0.81--1.06, and the sampler rewrites the action slot as a fixed input, so policy retention cannot be inferred directly from that output. We therefore do not count this diagnostic as positive mediation evidence; the AlphaBrain mechanism claim remains limited to the row-wise causal ladder, fixed-action cut, and alternate-action control.

The evidence does not establish that every instruction-conditioned world model has this vulnerability, that repair always degrades accuracy, or that native task performance is harmed. The current experiments use two released carriers, compatible public VAE encoding for AlphaBrain, frozen weights, and LIBERO trajectory windows; the decision result is an independent planner probe rather than a native controller attack, and the study does not yet include a trained goal-free AlphaBrain head or a successful clean closed-loop baseline.

We inspected the other local checkpoints before selecting the paper scope. A2World has an action-conditioning probe but no comparable legal task-instruction goal interface; FACT targets RoboTwin rather than the current LIBERO threat model; and TD-MPC2 does not expose the proposed goal-to-world action-token bus. They are therefore not presented as artificial third-carrier replications.
