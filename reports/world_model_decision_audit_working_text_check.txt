              From Forecast Sensitivity to Decision Evidence:
            A Working Audit of Pretrained World-Model Agents

                                  Goal-Bus research working notes

                                            15 October 2026


    Status: research working draft; not submission-ready. This document reconciles
    current evidence and counterevidence. No qualiﬁed general core security claim, novel defense,
    or numerical novelty rating is established. It is not a submission package or a statement that
    the research goal has been achieved. Historical results with retired codecs or protocols are
    excluded from the current result tables.

                                                 Abstract

          A changed world-model forecast need not be an incorrect physical prediction, and an in-
      correct prediction need not cause a worse decision. We audit these distinctions using existing
      pretrained-model experiments and real environment replays. We separate ﬁxed-belief interven-
      tional prediction from observational conditioning, identify the forecast consumer in each system,
      and retain actual utility separately from model scores. WorldAgen’s corrected prediction evi-
      dence has one independent episode and six correlated transitions; its frozen native policy does
      not rank the predicted futures. On a ﬁxed Cosmos Policy development panel, forecast-branch
      Goal replacement changes success from 29/30 to 28/30. Existing ﬁrst-candidate continuations
      identify one harmful ﬁrst selection, without establishing complete mediation. On one DINO-
      WM native planning root, an execution-equivalent forecast-request rewrite reduces cumulative
      dense return from 786.9985 to 702.1983, while several success-related measures improve; trusted
      input binding exactly restores the original trace. A subsequent two-mean, 30-control diagnos-
      tic ﬁnds better predictions under the rewrite, but the native objective applied to actual future
      features also selects the lower-reward action. These bounded eﬀects support a careful decision
      audit, not a general prediction-corruption attack, population attack-success rate, or established
      new security mechanism.


1     Introduction

The research question is whether a restricted change to the inputs used for world prediction can
alter action selection and reduce utility in the real environment. Answering it requires more than
forecast divergence. We need a valid prediction contract, a decision path that consumes the forecast,
and an outcome measured independently of the model’s own score. A proposed repair must also
be evaluated against the same task and its eﬀects on normal behavior.
This working draft organizes existing evidence around those requirements. It does not introduce a
new attack, rerun an experiment, or reinterpret retired metrics as current evidence. The present
records contain both local decision eﬀects and substantial counterevidence to a broad Goal-Bus
interpretation. We report both. In particular, a lower real return can coexist with better forecast

We distinguish three primary decision paths: WorldAgen’s separate prediction and frozen-policy

name for the NVIDIA Cosmos Policy checkpoint. The evidence ledger in Section 7 identiﬁes the
current records supporting every result below.


2     Related context and claim boundary

WorldAgen describes a task-agnostic prediction head alongside a task-conditioned policy and an
online adaptation pathway [1]. Its stated isolation contract motivates a system-speciﬁc audit; it
does not establish that every instruction-conditioned predictor must ignore instructions. DINO-
WM uses pretrained visual patch features for action-sequence planning toward goal observations [2].
Cosmos Policy generates actions, future states, and values within a video-model-based system [3].
These are architectural references, not independent replications of the local results below.
The separation between prediction accuracy and decision quality already has direct precedent.
Singh’s LeWorldModel/TwoRoom study changes the planning objective on frozen representations
and reports that the objective can limit planning even when prediction remains informative [4]. Our
local two-candidate diagnostic is consistent with that concern, but does not reproduce that study
or establish a new general phenomenon. TRAP’s published abstract already frames attacks around
candidate ranking in world-model planning [5]; we have not reproduced its backdoor experiments.
Neither ranking ﬂips nor proxy mismatch alone supplies a novelty claim for this work.


3     Prediction and intervention contracts

3.1   Physical invariance under a ﬁxed belief

Let H contain the complete model-visible history: observations, supplied proprioception, previous
actions, time indices, and relevant model cache state. Let S denote the augmented physical state,
including hidden simulator and controller state. A ﬁxed belief b(ds | H) need not be concentrated on
one state. Let A be a committed open-loop action sequence covering the entire evaluated horizon,
and let Es (A) denote its eﬀective executor controls.
If A and A′ have identical executor eﬀects for every possible and reachable augmented state, the
physical transition mechanism has no additional dependence on their raw representation, and the
conditional exogenous-noise law is the same, then their physical transition kernels agree. Conse-
quently, for a physical-outcome event B,
                                                ∫
                      Pb (Y ∈ B | H, do(A)) =       KH (B | s, Es (A)) b(ds | H)
                                                                                                 (1)
                                             = Pb (Y ∈ B | H, do(A′ )).
With paired exogenous noise, the corresponding trajectories can be compared sample by sample;
without it, equality concerns distributions. Partial observability does not invalidate Equation 1
when its ﬁxed-belief and executor assumptions hold. This is a standard conditioning and interven-
tion argument, not a proposed new theorem.

                                                    2
The contract includes the full duration and action sequence. Holding only a ﬁrst action, a high-
level instruction, or an observed feedback-generated sequence ﬁxed is insuﬃcient. State-dependent
executor logic must be included in Es or in the augmented state. A reward that penalizes raw
commands is also outside an equivalence claim based only on clipped controls.


3.2   Observational conditioning is a diﬀerent target

A predictor trained on behavior data can instead approximate
                                          ∫
                 Ptrain (Y | H, A, G) =       Ptrain (Y | s, H, A, G)Ptrain (ds | H, A, G).       (2)

Behavioral actions and instructions can reveal hidden state; a recorded feedback sequence can also
select future disturbances. Thus the conditional state/noise distribution may change when A or G
changes. Equality of physical transitions does not automatically impose an invariance obligation
on this observational conditional predictor. A ﬁxed evaluation belief and an external do(A) query
deﬁne a diﬀerent target; training support and the model’s intended prediction contract remain
separate questions.
For Goal replacement, we distinguish the original task Goal Gtask from a forecast-query ﬁeld Gquery .
The former determines the protected task, policy/value objective, and success metric. Physical
invariance under changes to the latter requires preference-only metadata: no change to reset, scene,
controller semantics, committed actions, physical target, or belief. Arbitrary alternative text need
not satisfy this condition. A legal instruction can convey hidden physical information. Randomly
crossing Goal texts in an evaluation does not establish their joint training support.
WorldAgen additionally has an explicit task-agnostic/world-head isolation claim that can be au-
dited as an implementation contract. AlphaBrain and Cosmos Policy do not inherit that promise
merely because they also predict future observations. Goal dependence by itself is therefore neither
a universal contract violation nor a successful attack. The current prediction-contract audit explic-
itly supersedes its earlier codec checklist with the corrected WorldAgen evidence and preserves this
observational boundary (E1).


4     Systems and methods

4.1   Native forecast consumers

WorldAgen: prediction is not native frozen-policy ranking.
The native policy call emits actions, while a separate world-model call uses action history to predict
images. The frozen policy does not consume these predicted futures through an action-ranking loop.
Test-time training through shared parameters is a diﬀerent pathway and must not be inferred from
a ﬁxed-forward prediction eﬀect. Historical CLIP-based selectors were added planners with retired
protocols; they are not used as current native decision evidence (E1, E5).

Cosmos Policy: generated candidate, conditioned future, predicted value.
The evaluated native-style best-of-N path generates an action candidate, predicts its future, evalu-
ates that future, and selects by predicted value. The intervention changes the Future branch’s text
while retaining the original Policy and Value text. Candidate seeds and controls are bound in the
saved records. The Torch compatibility adapter does not establish complete parity with the original

TransformerEngine execution. N = 1 still generates futures and values; it is not a world-model-free
baseline (E2, E5).

DINO-WM: predicted latent distance drives native CEM.
The locally evaluated checkpoint is the JEPA-WMs project’s released reproduction of the DINO-
WM baseline, not an unchanged installation of the original DINO-WM project. Its latent forecasts
are scored against the original encoded Goal. The recorded native planner uses 300 candidates,
15 iterations, 10 elites, horizon six macro-actions, and FP32. Each macro contains ﬁve physical
controls; three macros are normally executed before replanning. Goal enters the objective, not an
isolated Goal-conditioned forecast input. The tested operation is an action-representation rewrite,
not Goal-Swap (E3, E5).

AlphaBrain’s separate released checkpoint jointly denoises action and future slots. This establishes
shared computation, not an independently validated future-score-to-action selector. Its corrected
codec produced one successful LIBERO-Goal task0/init0 episode within a 300-control budget. A
subsequent nine-instance attempt stopped with CUDA OOM before any result; it is not 0/9 success.
These records do not substitute for the Cosmos panel (E5).


4.2   Interventions and protected quantities

For Cosmos, the ﬁxed development panel comprises ten tasks and three initial states per task. The
compared branch texts describe distinct legal Goals in the declared same scene, but this does not
prove that crossed conditional inputs are in training support. Existing candidate continuations
compute the original ﬁrst query, force one candidate index once, and use original-Goal planning
thereafter. An oﬄine join relates these measured branches to the ﬁrst selections in the Clean and
continuous-Swap episodes (E2).
For DINO, the researcher-added writer acts only on a copied forecast request. It rewrites satu-
rated XYZ representations while preserving their actual FP32-denormalized clipped controls and
the raw gripper command. Original CEM candidate tensors, Goal, context, weights, objective,
and execution path remain protected; the rewrite itself does not consume RNG. The physical
equivalence concerns two representations of each ﬁxed candidate. Diﬀerent planner conditions can
subsequently select diﬀerent candidates and therefore execute diﬀerent physical controls. There is
no contradiction between per-candidate equivalence and diﬀerent closed-loop trajectories (E3–E5).
The restoration condition uses trusted-current-candidate binding: a private original request is
supplied to the native predictor despite the writer’s modiﬁed copy. This is conventional input-
integrity restoration under the stipulated permission boundary. It is not a new defense or evidence
of an existing external low-trust writer in the native application. A researcher- deﬁned boundary
remains valid for a bounded causal experiment; its existence does not establish deployment exposure
(E3, E5).


4.3   Outcomes, pairing, and inference units

We keep forecast error, predicted cost, selected action, and actual physical utility separate. Cos-
mos success and capped completion cost come from real continuations. DINO reports cumulative
dense reward, terminal reward/distance, ﬁrst observed success, success occupancy, and ﬁnal success
separately. Predicted feature cost is never substituted for actual utility.


                                                 4
Forecast comparisons pair the same history, original action sequence, Goal, and real consequence
within a query. Comparing average errors along two diﬀerent visited trajectories is not a causal
pairing. DINO’s 14 selected-mean queries and 38 observed-preﬁx endpoints are correlated mea-
surements from one root pair, not independent task samples. Its earlier CPU diagnostic failed a
frozen numerical calibration gate and remains invalid; a separately registered same-GPU diagnos-
tic passed calibration before the paired comparisons. No tolerance relaxation is used to erase that
failed attempt (E3).


5     Veriﬁed results

5.1   WorldAgen: corrected prediction evidence has limited scope

The current archive records a 128-pixel native Clean conﬁguration with 21/30 successes. This
conﬁguration is distinct from earlier camera settings. The corrected physical-prediction evidence
contains one independent source episode and six correlated transitions. Mean native RGB-patch
MSE is 0.0554368 for Clean, 0.0678744 for same-scene Goal-Swap, 0.0551375 for Goal-Swap plus
Bus-Cut, 0.0678744 for the matched Control-Cut, and 0.0772741 for persistence. These descriptive
means are not independent episode estimates or decision utilities. They support a narrowly scoped
instruction- isolation and prediction audit, not an independent multi-episode result or native de-
cision harm. The archive’s task0 cached adaptation comparison records Frozen 1/3, no-language
adaptation 3/3, and swapped adaptation 3/3; it does not show a success loss from swapping. Nei-
ther this comparison nor the corrected forecasts supply the missing native forecast-ranking pathway
(E1, E5).
Old image-orientation, target-normalization, action-codec, and added-planner results are not in-
cluded in the current tables. In particular, an old target-MSE or latent-divergence value cannot be
promoted to calibrated physical forecast error merely because it was retained in a historical report.


5.2   Cosmos: one real failure and a bounded ﬁrst-selection link

The ﬁxed continuous Future-Goal intervention changes observed success from 29/30 to 28/30. This
is one lost Clean success, not a population attack-success estimate. The registered extension gate
remains failed. The existing-cache join ﬁnds three ﬁrst-selection changes; selecting the correspond-
ing measured original-Goal continuation for each root also yields 28/30 successes and an aggregate
capped-step increase of 130, or 4.3333 per root (E2).
The identiﬁed harmful root is task3/init2. Clean selects candidate1 and succeeds in 170 controls.
Candidate0, selected by Swap, fails within the 300-control cap even when subsequent planning uses
the original Goal. Saved candidate controls and the ﬁrst 16 physical controls bind these records.
Thus this ﬁrst choice is suﬃcient for the recorded failure under the measured continuation. This
is not proof that later Swap calls are irrelevant or that the ﬁrst choice fully mediates continuous
Swap. The join executes no new one-shot-Swap episode and no new recovery experiment (E2).
The 60 candidate continuations retain their provenance as 50 original branches plus ten recovery
branches. Native ﬁrst selection, ﬁxed candidate0, ﬁxed candidate1, and uniform selection in expec-
tation each achieve 29/30 on that panel; a candidate oracle achieves 30/30. Lack of a panel-wide
native selection advantage does not erase the local harmful selection, but it constrains broad claims
about the selector’s beneﬁt. No Bus-Cut, matched Control-Cut, or useful decision-recovery defense

5.3   DINO: native return loss and exact restoration, with mixed utility

The small Clean capability panel uses MuJoCo 3.3.0 and has ﬁnal success in 2/3 roots, versus 0/3
for no operation. A direct servo comparator has 0/3 ﬁnal success but reaches success at some point
in 3/3 and has higher cumulative return on all three roots. This establishes some usable Clean
capability, not dominance over the stronger comparator or robust task competence (E5).
Table 1 reports one native development root. Canonicalization reduces cumulative return by
84.800227, or 10.7751%, over the same 99 executed controls. Trusted binding restores return
exactly and all 99 saved trace rows match Clean. However, canonicalization reaches success earlier,
spends more controls in success, and ends closer to the target with higher terminal reward. Both
original arms have ﬁnal success false and ever-success true. These data do not establish a loss of
task success or uniform deterioration of utility (E3).
The paired preﬁx diagnostic ﬁnds lower average feature error under canonicalization within each
trajectory-origin panel, with mixed signs across queries and endpoints. This rejects a blanket
worse-average-prediction account; it does not identify the rankings of the full native CEM popu-
lations. The earlier ﬁxed-magnitude +2 operation remains a separate no-harm result and is not
retrospectively relabeled as the successful native intervention (E3).


5.4   Matched 30-control truth: the objective itself prefers lower utility

A post-hoc bank contains the two complete means returned at the ﬁrst native query: a0 from Clean
and a1 from canonical planning. They start from the same root and share the original context and
Goal. Within each candidate, only forecast-request representation changes. They are not the native
CEM population or two independent test cases.
Both full means were replayed for 30 physical controls. Their ﬁrst 15 controls exactly reproduce
the saved native preﬁxes; controls 16–30 are the committed open-loop suﬃx, whereas the native
agent would normally replan after 15. Three encoder calibrations passed exactly before two new
terminal-truth encodings. The diagnostic makes no new predictor or planner calls. It closes the
horizon mismatch for this bank without reconstructing the native 99-step decision process (E4).

This establishes objective/utility mismatch on this bank. It does not prove that prediction error has
no causal role: error direction can still change a ranking. Nor does it show what a fully accurate
world model would do throughout CEM, since diﬀerent forecasts would change subsequent proposals
and the candidate bank itself. It speciﬁcally rejects attribution to worsened terminal prediction
MSE for these two means. The physical-utility loss is real; calling it a distinct prediction-corruption
mechanism is unsupported (E4).


6    Limitations and current research decision

Inference units and selection. WorldAgen’s corrected prediction panel has one independent
episode; DINO’s native pair has one root, and its two-mean bank was chosen after the native
runs. Cosmos uses a ﬁxed development panel with one lost success. We report counts and paired
descriptive contrasts, not population ASR, generalization, independent replication, or statistical
signiﬁcance. Correlated steps, queries, branch replays, and repeated conditions do not increase the
number of independent physical roots.
Contract and support. Executor equivalence is not a proof of observational conditional invari-
ance, joint training support, or calibrated prediction. The Goal-Bus and action-alias interventions
have diﬀerent permissions and cannot be combined into one universal Goal vulnerability. Training-

Causality and utility. The Cosmos ﬁrst-selection join establishes a measured continuation con-
sequence, not full mediation or a Bus-Cut defense. The DINO truth diagnostic establishes a local
proxy mismatch, not complete CEM or closed-loop mediation. Better average MSE does not
guarantee better real decisions, and worse dense return does not automatically imply lost goal
attainment. The separate utility measures must remain visible.
Implementation and deployment. Checkpoint availability and a successful forward are not task
competence. Adapters, codecs, numerical backends, and renderer versions are part of each recorded
system. Technical errors and resource-deferred attempts are not task failures. The experimental
writer is a stated research permission, not an observed deployed adversary capability; ordinary
binding is not a novel defense.
Go/No-Go. Go for retaining the bounded causal eﬀects and their counterchecks: one Cosmos ﬁrst-
selection consequence, one DINO native dense-return loss with exact restoration, and calibrated
ﬁxed-action prediction/score diagnostics. No-Go for a qualiﬁed general core security mechanism,
useful general Bus-Cut defense, numerical novelty claim, or submission-ready paper. The present
evidence does not justify expanding the same aliases, adding seeds merely to seek a stronger eﬀect,
or presenting objective mismatch as a new security mechanism. A new experiment needs a distinct,
explicit operation and a control that can separate it from the existing explanations.
